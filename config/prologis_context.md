# Prologis context for the implication step

This file is injected into the implication prompt so that implications are
specific to Prologis rather than generic. Keep it factual and short. Update it
when Sarah shares internal guidance. Sources: Prologis 10-K FY2025, investor
materials, kickoff calls with Sarah Logman (2026-09-25, 2026-10-02).

## Business
- World's largest owner, developer and operator of logistics real estate: ~1.3B
  sq ft, ~$3.2T of goods flowing through the portfolio annually.
- Core business is leasing industrial buildings (fulfillment, distribution,
  last-mile, light industrial) to ~6,500 customers. Largest customers ("dark
  green" list) include Amazon, Walmart, DHL, PepsiCo, GXO, Home Depot, FedEx, UPS.
- Prologis Essentials (racking, solar, EV charging, energy storage) is ~3% of
  revenue, growing fast from a small base. Treat as a bonus, not the focus.
- Competitors: Rexford, EastGroup, First Industrial, STAG, Link Logistics,
  Segro, Goodman, plus private developers.

## Key markets
- US: Southern California (Inland Empire, LA), New Jersey / New York, Chicago,
  Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston, Atlanta, Pennsylvania (Lehigh Valley), Central
  Valley CA, Seattle, Phoenix, Las Vegas, South Florida.
- International: UK, Germany, Netherlands, France, Poland, Mexico (nearshoring),
  Japan, China, Brazil.
- Data center hubs to watch: Northern Virginia, Dallas, Phoenix, Columbus,
  Atlanta, and emerging markets where power is available. Prologis also pursues
  data center development on owned land where power can be secured.

## What Sarah's team cares about (from kickoff)
- Signal vs implication should be ~50/50; today it is too signal-heavy.
- Report structure: here's what's happening, why it matters, what it means for
  Prologis (and for you, the reader), what we're watching.
- Two horizons: short-term insights for field teams signing leases daily, and
  longer-term themes to watch at the executive level. Executives do not change
  strategy on a single customer's announcement.
- Hot topics: own vs lease (Amazon is still 90%+ leased despite press about an
  owned model; it owns strategically, not wholesale), data centers (hyperscalers
  and their suppliers: electrical, cooling, plumbing), 3PL consolidation and
  outsourcing, diesel prices and interest rates (so far limited impact).
- Be factual. No conjecture presented as fact. Always link the source.
