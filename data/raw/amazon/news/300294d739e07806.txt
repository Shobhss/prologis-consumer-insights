Amazon (NASDAQ:AMZN) is expanding the use of AI-powered smart glasses for delivery drivers, pushing wearable technology deeper into its last-mile logistics network. The move is aimed at shaving friction from one of the most expensive parts of e-commerce fulfillment by reducing reliance on smartphones and giving drivers package, navigation and safety information directly in their field of view.
The glasses use artificial intelligence, computer vision and cameras to guide drivers from their vehicles to delivery locations, identify possible hazards and help confirm that packages reach the correct address.
Amazon has already tested the technology with more than 500 delivery associates over the past 18 months. Those drivers completed more than 275,000 deliveries using the system.
The company now plans to deploy another 5,000 devices this year and have more than 20,000 in use by the end of 2027, turning what began as a relatively small trial into a much broader operational rollout.
The glasses activate after a driver safely parks and can display which packages are needed for the stop before providing walking directions to the destination. Amazon's geospatial technology is designed to help in more complicated environments such as apartment buildings, where finding the correct unit can add time to each delivery.
The devices can also warn drivers about potential pets, flag packages that appear to have been left at the wrong address and capture proof of delivery. A wearable controller includes a replaceable battery and emergency button.
Use remains voluntary for Amazon's delivery-service partners and their drivers.
Investor Takeaway
The financial significance will depend on whether the glasses measurably improve delivery productivity rather than simply adding another layer of technology. Investors should watch for evidence that Amazon can reduce time per stop, lower failed or incorrect deliveries and improve driver safety as deployment scales toward 20,000 units. Even small efficiency gains can matter across Amazon's enormous logistics network. The broader implication is that AI spending is moving beyond cloud infrastructure and consumer products into Amazon's physical operations, where productivity improvements could eventually support margins.
